How I learn

  1. Start with a problemUsually one I can see: my father's registers, a shopkeeper's credit notebook, a clinic nobody could find online.
  2. Find out what I don't knowDatabases, logins, payment gateways, the WhatsApp API. I read, watch, and ask a lot of questions, including to AI tools.
  3. Build the smallest versionSomething the person can actually try, even if it's rough.
  4. Watch it break, then fix itA security audit, a form that confused people, OCR that misread a word. Most of what I know came from this step.

How I work with AI

I use AI coding assistants, mostly Claude, to write a lot of my code. I'd rather be honest about that than pretend otherwise. What they don't do is decide what a clinic actually needs, choose how a database and WhatsApp should talk to each other, or notice that a manager can see records they shouldn't. That's the part I own, and it's the part I've learned the most from.

Beyond the code

Working on our family's PG and on Hitex Health Nest pulled me into the rest of the business: running Meta and ChatGPT ads for the PG, helping the clinic set up its Instagram and plan its first posts, and thinking about how a customer moves from finding a business to getting a reply. It showed me that a website is one part of a much bigger system.

Where I'm heading

An undergraduate degree in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning, aiming to start in 2027. I've used AI in real projects, like reading handwriting and answering questions offline. Now I want to understand properly how it works underneath.
